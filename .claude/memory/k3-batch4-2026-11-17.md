Kent, 2026-11-17: "Let's keep going". Batch 4 of the K3 logo trial
([[k3-logo-trial-2026-11-16]]): a cloud Sonnet session digitized ten more
Wikimedia Commons logos (NASA insignia, National Weather Service, Fish and
Wildlife Service, FedEx, Google, Microsoft, LEGO, DHL, Caterpillar, Subway;
80 or 100 mm, `left_chest`), the foreman judged the sheets by eye, local
Sonnet workers probed and built. None of the art or renders is in the repo.

**Shipped:** `enclosed_white_keeps_known_colour`
([[enclosed-known-colour-2026-11-17]]) and `border_by_size_corner_loss`
([[border-by-size-corner-loss-2026-11-17]]), both ON by Kent's "Flip ON".

**Parked, with what was measured (do not redo these):**

- **Mastercard's half-ringed circles are by design.** The size ring sews only
  where a shape meets bare fabric (`border_by_size_fabric_edges`, Kent ON
  10-28): whole round the two-circle silhouette, skipped on the lens seam
  (ring fractions 0.71 / 0.71 / lens 0). Fabric-edge rule off rings the lens
  and reads worse. Visa (0.68) and Texaco (0.455, 0.458) are partial the same
  way.
- **USAID "FROH" has no measure.** The small-text re-set scales to a 4.0 mm
  cap, condenses to 0.80 and widens strokes 0.24 mm with
  `_widen_keep_counters`, which guards holes only; the M's shallow V (0.42 of
  cap) fills (centre solid 0.17 -> 0.35 of cap). Over 208 letter bays in 8
  re-set designs the M's notch ranks 119-120 on share lost (0.52), and
  mid-pack on open width and depth: a notch guard would hit dozens of letters
  that read fine. WIP on origin: `claude/reset-keeps-notches` (flag plumbing
  and `_notch_census`, OFF, no PR).
- **"No size ring on a sharp-cornered shape" contradicts the pro.**
  `tools/border_census.py` re-run, shapes >= 6 mm: the pro rings 26 of 46
  sharp-cornered shapes (Becker's block letters and badge, MFab's letters,
  Drone's body) and 3 of 7 smooth ones. Built, not shipped:
  `claude/border-by-size-smooth-only` (corner detector + 23 tests). So ringed
  block letters (DHL, FedEx) are the pro's habit. A ring at radius 0 reads as
  a picture frame.
- **The pro's ring keeps the corner tighter than ours.** On Becker (13
  corners, one art at three widths) the pro's outer needle points recede by
  an equivalent radius of 0.47 mm where ours give 2.32 (the cushion on
  Microsoft's 10 mm squares); the pro still fans, and holds 2.25 mm width at
  the corner where ours pinches to 1.59. MFab and Drone corners could not be
  measured. Unfinished WIP on origin: `claude/border-by-size-tight-corner`
  (`border_by_size_tight_corner`, radius 0.5 for by-size rings only; no
  census, no sheets, tests not run).
- **Fish and Wildlife's heading sews half bean, half satin.**
  `thin_ink.bean_dark_ink` beans the letters stage 2 tagged `dark_ink` (S, &,
  W, V) while their word-mates sew satin; widths overlap (0.60-0.76 against
  0.59-0.86), the tag decides. `dark_ink_bean=false` makes the word
  consistent but un-beans pen-line ring text elsewhere. Unfinished WIP on
  origin: `claude/dark-ink-bean-word-tier` (a dark-ink letter that is a
  minority of its text cluster is not beaned; no census, no sheets, tests not
  run).
- **Bold caps damaged by the small-text path (not traced).** In the same
  heading (cap 4.3 mm, strokes 0.6-0.9 mm) the F sews as a "Γ" and a C as a
  "Г"; `small_text_cap_mm=null` or `lettering_columns=false` repairs them.
  The probe was lost when the session ended. Likely the FROH family.
- Not faults of ours: Fish and Wildlife's sun merges into the sky because the
  Studio asks for 6 colours and the art has 7 (at 7 it returns); DHL's yellow
  field is read as the page; LEGO's thin black frame is the page flood and
  nothing sews it; Subway's fans are thick one-spine satin letters (the
  Texaco star family); the meatball's stars are under what thread can draw.

**Traps:**

- A cloud session on the Default environment cannot reach Commons (the
  proxy refuses `commons.wikimedia.org`). Start the worker on, or move it
  to, the `emb-bot-commons-corpus` environment. Commons then answers about
  half the requests with 429; the tool's `fetch` waits and retries.
- A builder told to check the pro census BEFORE shipping stopped two rules
  the foreman's eye had already chosen. Put that check in every brief that
  changes a layout habit.
- Unticking "Sew white areas" cannot rescue LEGO-type art:
  `white_keyline_art.detect` turns `sew_enclosed_white` back on in
  `finish_generation`.
- An app restart stops in-process workers and leaves their lanes
  uncommitted. Have builders commit WIP to their branch as they go.
