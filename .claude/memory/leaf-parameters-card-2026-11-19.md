**What Kent sent (2026-11-19, "make note of this").** A labelled picture of a
sewn green leaf, titled "Digitizing leaf parameters | Embroidery technical
study". It names six parameters a digitizer sets for one object:

| Parameter | Value on the card |
|---|---|
| Stitch direction | 150 deg; the sewn halves actually slant into the centre vein, a different angle each side |
| Density | 4.0 mm stitch length, 0.40 mm spacing |
| Pull compensation | +0.30 mm width along the edges |
| Underlay | edge walk and light zigzag |
| Start point | needle entry at the base of the stem |
| End point | thread trim at the leaf tip |

The vein is a raised run/satin line down the middle, the stem a narrow satin
column, and the leaf sews from the stem out to the tip, so it ends where it trims.

**Treat it as a probable AI mock-up.** Leftover typesetting notes are printed
on the card ("Helvetica Neue, 10pt"), one label is garbled ("appovs:on"), and
"edges" is doubled. Same trap as about a third of the service-site pictures in
[[digitized-image-study-2026-11-13]].

**Why:** Kent wants it on record as a reference for how a single natural shape
is specified: direction, density, pull comp, underlay, entry and exit.

**How to apply:** use it as a checklist of the parameters a leaf (or any split
shape with a midrib) needs, and as a picture of the split-direction habit
(each half angled into the vein). None of its numbers may become a constant:
it is not a sew-out (ROADMAP gate 1), and pull comp, spacing and underlay are
already set per fabric in the digitizer. To compare, measure our output on a
leaf fixture against a real professional file, not against this card.
