Prime grader 11-17: the h's stem "built from offset segments with visible steps" (`toat_machine_hat`).

**Cause (measured):** script words go through `script_strokes.script_columns` (pen-stroke skeleton, crosses cast to
the ink), then `columns.column_runs`. The stem is ONE `curved` column of 61 stations; where the c's tail and the
arch's hairline merge into it the ART is wider, so its cross runs 3.1-3.4 against 2.3-2.5 with a step at each end.
`stage6_satin.satin_stroke` is NOT the stem's sewer: it only runs in stage 7's lane-OFF ordering pass, so every
`_rail_points` change (gap hold, envelope, ramp, bulge cap) was inert on the plan (final stem byte-identical).
The grader's SEAM (pins y 35.0 / 39.5) was a second thing: at each wedge end the skeleton's join kink turns the cross,
a rail point sits 0.1-0.2 mm off its pitch and the stagger shortens that cross into a notch (1.9 mm against 2.9-3.0).

**Fix (`cfg.lettering_columns_stem_wedge_cap`, `wedge_cap.py`; ON since 11-18, Kent "Flip ON (Recommended)"):** the
thicker column gives a wedge back (opened width profile, a 0.3 mm step at both ends) only to a sibling that is a
HAIRLINE join (median cross under 0.6 of its own, `THINNER`) whose piece already covers the ground, siblings read as
already capped; trimmed rail points go ON the chord and the points within 3 stations of a wedge end are re-spaced
evenly. Evidence in `docs/renders/wedge-cap-2026-11-18/`: 44 runs (18 tripwire placements + 26 fixtures), the hat is
the only mover (2 stitches), bare 0 -> 0, `satin_cover` 0.9954 -> 0.9957.

**Traps:** (1) the first rule (any covering sibling, then 0.85) let the thin c-tail column give its ground across
the stem to the stem, and at 0.85 Bridge's g-to-e link (a sibling at 0.65, 2 degrees) read as a wedge. 0.6 sits
between two measured ratios (0.49 the h, 0.65 Bridge), two examples. (2) A push ramp in `_push_rails` cleared the
notch but reached every lane column (18 of 35 moved, cover losses to 0.046): dropped. (3) The h's top cap is a separate
fault and stays.
