## What the walk does now (`digitizer/digitizer_core/columns.py`)

- **A tip's two ends are read off the cut, not off the merge radius**
  (`_apex_tip_columns`, `_span_graph`'s `tips`). The radius (1.3 W + 0.3 + pull)
  gave a tip's cut node to a neighbouring stub, which left MFab's W in three
  components with a trim between them. On a bold letter it also joined a blunt
  top to a leg's foot 4 mm away: PROSEAL's A was sewn up into its top, then
  hopped 3.3 mm back down over it. A blunt tip counts as a tip only where the
  radius would join it like that; a free stub (MFab's 4) stays a dead end the
  walk doubles. A cross split at its crotch counts as one line.
- **A tip's point may start a trail but never end one.** Trail ends and doubled
  paths are chosen together, by millimetres (`_euler_trail`'s `postman`, a
  minimum-weight matching). The old pairing doubled 29 mm of strokes on MARINE's
  M at 95.7 mm to pair a 3 mm tip.
- **Out of a tip square to its cut.** The exit hop runs along an offset when the
  straight way would skim the tip.
- **No tuck across a tip** (`_tuck_stations`' `avoid`). Today's M at 95.7 mm laid
  3.4 mm of its right leg's satin over the V.
- **Every leg of a cut letter ends on the rail whose hop lies least on bare
  fabric, then least outside the satin still to come, then is shorter**, read on
  the legs' stations. The cut re-pieces the whole letter, and the rail a leg ends
  on followed its station count: MFab's 4 ran a 1.7 mm bare hop down its corner.
  Read on the column pieces, the rule took PROSEAL's small N's shorter hop across
  a notch no stitch reaches; read on stations, it does not.
- **A hop keeps off a tip sewn before it.** It steps off square and back
  (MFab's IN: 0.12 mm along the N's foot).

## How to reproduce

```bash
cd digitizer
python -m tools.apex_walk_census --cache DIR gen  JOB          # from a main checkout
python -m tools.apex_walk_census --cache DIR arm  JOB off|on   # main's engine
python -m tools.apex_walk_census --cache DIR arm  JOB on_new   # this branch
python -m tools.apex_walk_census --cache DIR arm  JOB off_new  # this branch: OFF's md5
python -m tools.apex_walk_census --cache DIR table --out table.md
python -m tools.apex_walk_census --cache DIR render --out DIR/renders
python -m tools.apex_walk_census --cache DIR fixture --out testdata/apex_walk_letters.json   # from main
```

The arms ran on Kent's laptop under the wave CPU lock, at
`photo_prep_background_removal_timeout_s=900`, with rembg available on every arm.
The census's split reader fuses split stitches at 1e-3 mm. An arm stores its
runs to 1e-4 mm, so `strip_splits`' 1e-6 found none, and a plan's split satin
read its half stitches as crosses (PROSEAL's A at 85%: 15.3 vs the run's own
10.1).

`digitizer/tests/test_column_apex_walk.py` holds the bar on the 12 tipped
