# How Kent judges a pair: the calibrated rubric

This text is appended, unchanged, to the judge's PROMPT. Every rule below comes
from Kent's own one-line reasons, written 2026-11-07 on the 22 CALIBRATION
pairs (kit ids C01 to C22). His words are quoted exactly, spelling included.
No rule here stands without a quote from him behind it.

QUESTION: Which of the two stitch renders, LEFT or RIGHT, would Kent rather
sew? Decide it on the LETTERING first. Do the stitches in each letter follow
the way a pen writes that letter? Does each letter keep its true shape, with
smooth curves and straight strokes? Then check the rest of the art. Does any
part break off, or carry a stray crossing stitch or a blob? Answer tie only
when both renders fail in the same way, or nothing between them differs.

## Where to look

Look at the letters before anything else. Twelve of the fourteen reasons that
give a cause name a letter or "the lettering". Find the letters where LEFT and
RIGHT differ, and judge the pair there. A pair is not a tie just because the
rest of the design is the same.

## The principles, most important first

### 1. The stitches follow the pen stroke

Kent judges a letter by "the rules of penmanship". The stitch direction should
run the way a pen or pencil moves through that letter. It should turn with the
stroke and never cut across it.

- "the right one is better becuase the stitching follows the flow of the "R".
  The left one has that straight satin stitching across the top the breaks up
  the flow." (C01)
- "Right is better, the letters follow the rules of penmenship." (C12)
- "notice how the stitching doesnt flow like the rules of penmenship on the
  "h"." (C21)
- "I choose left, because of the stitching on the "A" has better flow." (C19)
- "They both suck, they don;t follow the rules of writting letters with a
  pen/pencil." (C03)

A band of stitches that runs straight across a curve "breaks up the flow". So
does a patch whose direction jumps where two strokes meet. Either one loses to
a letter whose stitches keep turning with the stroke.

### 2. Curves are smooth, with no flat spots

A round letter should be round. A flat facet, a chamfer or a sudden corner
cut into a curve is a defect, even when everything else matches.

- "the "P" flows smoothly, and does not have the abrubt flat spot like the
  right "P"" (C09)
- "Left is better the "O" is smoother and has better flow." (C08)
- "Left is better. much smoother lettering." (C15)

### 3. Straight strokes stay straight, at the angle the letter is drawn

An upright should be straight, not wavy. A stroke the letter draws level
should not come out on a slant.

- "Right is better, notice how straight the "I" is, it;s not wavey like the
  left one." (C05)
- "Right is better for letter shape (the left side made the top of the "L"s
  on an angle, which is wrong)." (C17)

### 4. Letter shape outweighs spacing

When one render spaces the letters better and the other shapes them better,
the better letter shape wins.

- "The left is better w/ spacing between letters, but the right is better for
  lettering shape" (C10). His pick on that pair was RIGHT.
- "Right is better for letter shape" (C17)

### 5. No stray stitching: no crossover stitches, no blobs

A stitch that crosses where nothing is drawn is a defect. So is a lump of
stitching sitting inside a letter.

- "Left is better, that cross over stitch on the right one is wierd." (C11)
- "Left is better, the circle blob of stitching in the middle of the "M" is
  wierd" (C16)

### 6. The art holds together

A piece of the design that breaks off, or comes apart from the shape it
belongs to, loses to one that stays whole.

- "Left is better, the right anchor breaks off on the left side." (C13)

## When it is a tie, and when it is not

- **Both bad in the same way is a tie.** "These both suck." (C22). "They both
  suck, they don;t follow the rules of writting letters with a pen/pencil."
  (C03)
- **Both poor is NOT a tie when one is less poor.** "They are bothe poor, but
  the right one is better becuase the stitching follows the flow of the "R"."
  (C01). If both are flawed but one breaks fewer of the principles above, pick
  that one.
- **Nearly the same is NOT a tie when you can see a difference.** "Right is
  better, but these are damn near the same." (C06). A small difference in one
  letter, such as a straight "I" against a wavy one (C05), is enough to pick a
  side.
- After his reasons, Kent called a tie on 2 of these 22 pairs, and both
  times it was because both were bad. On the other 20 he picked a side,
  including pairs where an earlier judge saw "Nothing to choose".
