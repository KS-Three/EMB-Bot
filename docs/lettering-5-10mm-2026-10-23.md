## How it was read

One scratch reader over both DSTs (`band_measure.py`, in the session's
scratchpad — a copy of its rules follows so the next person re-measures):

- **Letters**: `tools.small_text_columns.pieces` (needle-down stitches drawn
  0.4 mm wide, opened 1.0 mm here, connected pieces), pieces with their
  centre in the word's band, 4 to 12.5 mm tall, no bar wider than 3x its
  height. Ours is cut into the pro's letter boxes (x spans mapped by the
  band's extent), so a fused "OTE" on one side is three letters on both.
- **Columns**: stitches of 0.5 mm or more; consecutive ones anti-parallel
  (dot under -0.2) and within 0.5-2x in length make a segment, with up to
  two short stitches (under 0.5 mm, the pro's inner-rail short stitch)
  skipped between them; six crosses make a column. Rails are the cross ends
  by parity.
- **Sewn width**: median cross length on the letter (p90 beside it).
- **Rail wobble**: `tools/letter_band.py`'s polygon-free jitter — a line
  through nine same-rail stations and the middle one's residual; median and
  p90 per letter. `tools/edge_wobble.py` needs a polygon the pro's file does
  not have, so it is not the instrument here.
- **Curve outer / inner pitch**: where the column's centreline turns 8 deg/mm
  or more (a bend, not a corner), the station spacing on the outer and the
  inner rail; n = crosses on bends.
- **Fan ends / ends**: an end (first or last 1.5 mm of a column) whose
  crosses turn more than 20 deg from the column's middle, per parity.
- **Junction butts (under, gap)**: a column end within 0.8 W of another
  column's body; "under" when it was sewn BEFORE that body (a tuck);
  "gap" when the 0.4 mm thread covers under 90% of a half-width disc at the
  meeting point. Folds (one column turning a corner) stay inside one segment
  and are not a junction.
- **Underlay**: needle-down thread on the letter that is not a satin cross;
  kind by where it lies (centre: over 60% of it in the middle 45% of the
  stroke; edge/zigzag: over half of it near the rails).
- **Word**: trims = needle-up groups beside a run that touches the word;
  needle-up = their jump length; travel = a word run's stitches with neither
  end on a letter; stitches = the word's runs.

Renders in `docs/renders/lettering-5-10mm-2026-10-23/`: `<job>_paths_ours_over_pro.png`
is the word as needle paths (1 px lines, red needle points, 40 px/mm), ours
above the pro; the per-letter crops are 60 px/mm.

## What the table says

- **Width is the pro's.** Every word within 0.1 mm per letter (Fremont 1.40
  vs 1.40, Drone 1.9 vs 1.9-2.0, Proseal 2.9 vs 2.9, MFab 1.5-1.8 vs 1.4-1.7).
  Not a lever in this band (as at 4 mm: `lettering-thickness-fremont-2026-10-06`).
- **Curve outer pitch is the pro's** (0.40-0.42 vs 0.36-0.41; #650 closed
  that). The pro short-stitches the INNER rail of a tight ring (Fremont's O
  inner 0.22 vs our 0.45); ours does not. Cosmetic at 7 mm.
- **Rail wobble**: Fremont's O p90 0.42 vs the pro's 0.14; MFab 0.25-0.40 vs
  0.02-0.32; Drone and Proseal ours is the lower. Not one-directional.
- **Construction**: ours 156 columns to the pro's 134 over 38 letters, 242
  butts to 195, 75 gaps to 54, fan ends 69% to 65% of ends. The pro FOLDS one
  column round a letter's corner (crosses pivot on the inner corner: Drone's
  C, E, S, O; Proseal's O corners), ours cuts the letter into straight columns
  and butts them, which is where the seams, the Drone S's six pieces and most
  of the extra trims come from (Drone 17 to 9; MFab 14 to 10).
- **Diagonals**: the pro sews an N's or M's diagonal with crosses nearly
  square to the LINE OF TEXT, like the stems (Fremont N, M; MFab N); ours
  sews them square to the diagonal. The 09-03 convention
  (`stitch-angle-convention-2026-09-03`) is the Column lane's rule and this
  band's letters do not go through that lane.
- **Underlay**: both sides a centre run under every column.
- **MFab's W** (`mfab_W_ours_pro.png`) is a tangle because a diagonal shape
  crosses the letters in our segmentation; the pro's W is four clean columns.
  Segmentation, not construction: a follow-up.

## The gap that costs legibility most

**A transparent counter cut into two colour regions sews shut.** Drone's
PRECISION at 62.6 mm: the O reads as a solid blob
(`drone_O_before_after_pro.png`, thread render; `drone_O_S_E_ours_pro.png`,
needle paths). Everything else in the table is seams, fans and trims a reader
still reads through; a 6.3 mm O with no counter is a different letter.

Mechanism, measured on the probe's regions: the O's polygon (region 48)
CARRIES its counter, 4.1 x 3.4 mm; two regions of 7.1 and 4.9 mm² sit inside
it, in the letter's own thread (`color_cap_merged_from` 3770 and 0142, both
capped into Silver 3971), both stitched. The art's counter pixels are fully
transparent (alpha mean 0.0). `alpha_edge_extend` (ON) paints a transparent
pixel with the nearest opaque colour, so the counter is painted light from
the letter's top edge and dark from its chrome bottom edge and vectorizes as
two regions. `tag_enclosed_background` tags a region only when it is mostly
one transparent component AND that component is mostly the region; the
second ratio reads 0.51 and 0.36 against 0.6, so both fail OPEN and sew. The
P's and R's counters are one region each, tagged `enclosed_background` +
`enclosed_colour_unknown`, unstitched: they stay open.

**Built: `cfg.enclosed_alpha_hole_parts` (OFF).** On the alpha path a region
is tagged when IT is mostly inside one transparent component (the first ratio
alone); the colour path keeps both ratios. Nothing with a colour of its own
sits under alpha < 128, so the second ratio protects nothing there. Layout,
not a fabric number: ON-eligible from the renders (Kent's ruling 2026-10-22).
Tests: `tests/test_enclosed_alpha_hole_parts.py` — a two-tone ring with a
transparent middle reproduces the defect OFF (a hole part sews) and is cured
ON (every part tagged, colour unknown, unstitched; the ring itself sews;
`logo_whitebg.png`'s colour-found hole unchanged).

## Follow-ups (not built here)

1. **Fold columns round corners** instead of cutting and butting — the pro's
   construction on every job in the band; the Drone S (6 pieces, ours) and the
   C/E corners are the crops. The Column lane's `find_cuts` is where a corner
   would become a fold rather than a cut; this band's letters do not go
   through that lane today (`lettering_columns` OFF above the small-bold
   gate).
