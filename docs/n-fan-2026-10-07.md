# The N fan: a sharp lettering junction read as a tapered tip (2026-10-07)

**Status: diagnosed, measured four ways, NOT fixed in the skeleton tier —
on purpose.** MASTER_SCOPE defect 59 (Kent, on the `letterform_priors`
sitting: *the "N" was better before - everything else was better after*).
Lane `claude/n-fan`, cut from the flip lane's head (`origin/main` after
#655 "Lettering as Columns", plus the `letterform_priors_k` ON commits).
Nothing in the engine changed on this lane; the instrument is committed as
`digitizer/tools/fan_census.py`. Renders and census JSON are in the lane's
gitignored `scratch_n_fan/` (client artwork).

## What the fan is, with the engine's own instruments

Becker's N at the corpus's 100 mm, priors ON. The refit outline is 13
vertices (two straight stems, one straight diagonal, two 78-degree notches
where the diagonal meets the stems); the trace was 40 vertices with 106-
and 155-degree rounded notches.

1. **The skeleton.** On the trace the N's medial axis has 9 edges — two
   corner forks into the rounded notches (removed by `_corner_forks`, tip
   dt 0.17) and short connectors — and welds are found at both nodes. On
   the refit it has 5 edges, no forks, exactly three arms at each node, and
   the junction stack refuses the through-weld at both (turn 37 deg by the
   merge's baseline, over `_STACK_WELD_TURN_DEG` 30; `tools/weld_turns.py`
   reads 36.2 / 36.9 on the trace and 37.0 on the refit — the same
   neighbourhood, not a window just crossed). So on both outlines the arms
   END at the node as junction ends. *(spy on `_corner_forks` /
   `_extend_to_cap`, `scratch_n_fan/fork_spy.log`)*

2. **The tip test is what changed.** `satin_tip_caps` (ON since
   2026-09-29, defect 49) asks every junction end with no single owner
   whether it is a tapered TIP: `_is_tip_end` casts a ray along the end's
   own tangent for `_TIP_REACH_HALVES` = 1.6 sewn half-widths and calls it
   a tip if the boundary is inside. On the **traced N all five junction
   ends read "meeting"** (no boundary within 4.36 mm). On the **refit N
   three of five read "TIP"**: the neighbouring stroke's far edge sits
   **4.56, 4.79 and 4.81 mm** along the end tangent, against a reach of
   **4.84 mm** (1.6 x 3.03). *(spy on `_is_tip_end`, `scratch_n_fan/tip_spy.log`)*

3. **A tip is capped, so the arm runs out to the edge** — `_extend_to_cap`,
   4.56–4.81 mm, THROUGH the neighbouring stroke: the diagonal's end at the
   lower node is pushed horizontally to the right stem's far edge (x 27.6 →
   32.2), the right stem's lower arm into the diagonal's body (27.6, 23.9 →
   24.5, 27.6), the diagonal's top end up through the left stem's root to
   the top edge (21.4, 20.6 → 24.5, 16.9). Each extended column's crosses
   span both strokes (up to 2.24 W, 11.7 mm on a 5.2 mm stroke) and rotate
   with the spine's bend into the node (97 → 135 deg over twelve crosses):
   the fan. Per run, crosses over the bare 1.6 W bar: the diagonal 42 / 20,
   the left stem 61 / 14, the right stem's two arms 26 / 4 and 53 / 7 — 45
   of 213; over the pull-aware bar of the census (1.6 x (W + 0.6)), 22 of
   213. On the trace: 118 crosses, 11 over 1.6 W and 1 over the pull-aware
   bar, the diagonal sewn node to node, square.
   *(`scratch_n_fan/n_off_on.log`; `tools/n_fan_spies.py --runs`)*

4. **Half the fan is the tip test; the other half is the junction corridor.**
   `satin_tip_caps=False` on the refit N: 213 → 158 crosses, over-long 45 →
   24, but satin bare on the N 4.85 → 5.92% (the capped ends were covering
   the wedge). The remaining 24 are the node's omnidirectional clearance —
   `half_at(node)` 4.5 mm against the arms' 3.0–3.5 (the weld tool's
   `node_r` / `arm_half`) — plus the stack's part-B reach into the node;
   the trace carries 11 of the same. *(`scratch_n_fan/n_tipcaps.log`,
   `welds_off.json` / `welds_on.json`)*

So the N is the repo's classic case: a gate set on one population
(`_TIP_REACH_HALVES` 1.6 — "the A's apex reads about one; an arm ending in a
letterform's corner ball has to cross the whole ball and reads several times
this") applied to another (a refit letter's sharp junction, where the
neighbouring stroke's far edge lies ~1.5 half-widths along the arm's
tangent). The outline is not the cause: the cleaner it is, the surer the
ray hits.

## What was not built, and why

No fix in the skeleton tier. The one that fits today's construction is
clear enough to name — `_is_tip_end` should not be asked at a node of three
arms (a tapered tip is where TWO arms meet, the A's apex; three strokes
cannot meet at a point), which means carrying the node's degree on the
`Stroke` and gating the tip test on it — but `lettering_columns` (#655,
built OFF, Kent's pick) takes the N to 62 square crosses and 0 over-long by
not running this tier at all, and the brief's rule is that a fix nobody
will sew is not worth building. Whether the skeleton tier gets the
degree gate (for the untagged shapes it will keep sewing, and for text if
Columns stays OFF) is Kent's call; the measurement that would price it is
one `satin_tip_caps` arm with the degree gate on the seven logos, bare area
included, and this lane's instrument is ready for it.

`satin_join_square` and this defect do not collide: it reaches slab and L
corners (`_split_sharp_corners` members) and leaves the N untouched, so the
serif lane's flip neither fixes nor worsens 59.


`tools/n_fan_spies.py` is the committed form of the spies that found the
mechanism: each monkeypatches one `stage6_satin` function during
`plan_stitches` on the chosen letter and prints what it was asked and what
it answered (`--tips` for `_is_tip_end`, `--ends` for `_corner_forks` and
`_extend_to_cap`, `--runs` for the per-run crosses). The scratch originals
(`fork_spy.py`, `tip_spy.py`, `n_runs.py`, `n_off_on.py`, `n_tipcaps.py`)
stay in the lane's `scratch_n_fan/` with their logs.
