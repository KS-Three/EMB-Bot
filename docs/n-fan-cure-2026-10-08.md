## The gate #661 named does not work

The 10-07 write-up proposed carrying the node's DEGREE and not asking the
tip test at a node of three arms ("a tapered tip is where TWO arms meet").
I measured that before building it, with a spy on `_is_tip_end` that reads
the caller's `Stroke`:

- **becker:** 59 asks, every one at a node of 3 or 4 arms (43 + 2 tips);
- **enthusiast:** 12 asks, all at 3 arms, all tips.

A node of two arms never reaches the test. `_merge_through_junctions` gives
one arm the corner as a cap and tucks the other under it, so `under` is set
and the tip branch is skipped. The degree gate is therefore
`satin_tip_caps=False` under another name. Two other scalar readings also
failed to separate the N from the rest:

|---|---|---|
| hit distance / sewn half-width | 1.51, 1.58, 1.59 | up to 1.53 (becker), 1.45 (drone) |
| hit distance / DT at the node | 1.01–1.06 | 0.03–1.27 |
| arm lengths at the node (half-widths) | 0.9–2.9 | overlapping |

## What the tips actually are

I drew every tip ray over the artwork for Becker's MARINE and ENTHUSIAST's
wordmark, with priors on and off. The renders hold client artwork, so they
stay in the lane's scratchpad. Nearly every red ray is a **meeting**:

- a T's stem driving up through the bar;
- the E's and H's middle bar driving through the stem;
- the A's crossbar driving through a leg;
- the N's diagonal driving through a stem.

Each ray lands on the far WALL of the stroke it crosses. ENTHUSIAST's A apex,
the case `satin_tip_caps` was built for (defect 49), is never asked at all.
`tests/test_satin_tip_caps.py` already said so: both its arms tuck.

So the population difference is not depth. It is **what the outline does
where the ray lands**. At a taper the outline closes round the end, which is
a convex corner. At a meeting the ray lands on a straight wall or in a
concave notch between two strokes.

`tools/tip_corner_census.py` prints the gate's own reading,
`_tip_corner_angle`. It is the interior angle of the outline at the hit,
over one sewn half-width of boundary either side, taken on the ink side (the
chord is inside the shape at a convex corner and outside it at a concave
one). One more rule applies: if the sharpest point in that window is more
than a quarter half-width from the hit, the window has caught a corner
BESIDE a wall hit, and the reading is the wall's (180).

| logo (corpus width) | tips | convex ≤ 160° (kept) | 160–179° | wall, 180° | notch, over 180° |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| becker 100 | 45 | 0 | 5 | 12 | 28 |
| enthusiast 80 | 12 | 0 | 1 | 9 | 2 |
| drone 80 | 63 | 3 (109, 140, 140) | 5 | 30 | 25 |
| fremont 92.5 | 47 | 0 | 4 | 31 | 12 |
| gaulke 80 | 41 | 0 | 5 | 15 | 21 |

**The review correction.** The first build read the angle at the hit only.
A wall hit within a half-width of a corner then read as convex: the
reviewer's probe, a 90° end 0.3 mm from the hit, read 113°. That first
build kept 16 ends (drone 12, becker 2 at 149° and 152°, fremont 2). Read
with the corner located, **13 of the 16 were wall hits beside a corner**.
Of the 3 that survive (drone), I drew all three:

- one is plainly a taper: the pointed foot of the bracket under the right
  arm, ray straight into the point;
- one is at an arm-junction corner and is ambiguous;
- the third does not resolve at that scale.

Readings on the fan letters:

- Becker's N: **180, 180, 180**.
- Becker's M: **278, 286, 297**. Its middle node's rays land in the V notch.
- Becker's E: **180**.

**160** was set on the first build's readings, which left a gap somewhere
in 152–164° on every logo. On ENTHUSIAST and gaulke, that only means it sat
below their lowest reading. On the corrected reading, the nearest values on
either side are 140 (drone, kept) and the 160–179 band (5 per logo at most,
none at or under 160). It was chosen on the same five logos it is judged
on. It is a geometric constant, and no machine settles it, so gate 1 does
not apply.

## The construction

`_tip_lands_on_corner(spine, poly, half_sewn, at_start)` in
`stage6_satin.py`, called only after `_is_tip_end` has said yes, and only
under the flag:

```
if tip_caps and under is None and _is_tip_end(...)
        and (not tip_corner_gate or _tip_lands_on_corner(...)):
    cap it
```

An end that fails falls through to the junction tuck that every other
meeting already gets. The flag is threaded beside `tip_caps` through
`stage7_sequence` → the shape emitter → `satin_stroke` → `_satin_joined`.

## What this says, plainly

**On this corpus the gate is almost exactly `satin_tip_caps=False`.** On
becker, gaulke, enthusiast, fremont and tires it is identical in stitches and
bare. On drone and bridge it is within 0.2 mm² and 80 stitches. Its whole
difference is the three drone ends it keeps. The coverage `satin_tip_caps` bought when it was flipped
(2026-09-29: bare down on all nine logos) was bought mostly at MEETINGS: arms
driven through the neighbouring stroke to its far wall. The N's fan is the
price of that same move on a refit letter's slanted junction.

So the choice behind the flag is coverage against construction:

- **+8.4 mm² of bare** over seven logos (+0.6 on Becker's letters);
- against **half the over-long crosses** and every fan;
- for 0.6–11% fewer stitches.

The gate is the narrow version of turning tip caps off: it keeps a taper's
apex capped, which is what the flag was built for.

`lettering_columns` (#655) still owns the full cure for TEXT: becker 56 → 0
over-long by not running this tier at all. This gate matters for the shapes
the skeleton tier keeps sewing (untagged artwork, and text if Columns stays
OFF).
